Everything you need from a map

From everyday errands around town to help in an emergency and a day in the mountains. No sign-in, no paid features and no pop-up ads.

The map, place search and directions work worldwide. Open Maps is made in Poland, and some of its data comes from Polish public services, so a few features cover Poland only. They are marked Poland below.

Search

Find what you're looking for

One box for everything: a place name, an address, coordinates or a Plus Code. Or just say what you're looking for.

  • Suggestions after just a few letters
  • Voice search: tap the microphone in the search box (in browsers with speech recognition)
  • Restaurants, shops, pharmacies and petrol stations nearby
  • Place cards with opening hours, phone number, website and weather
  • “About this place” and Wikipedia articles nearby, plus street-level photos from Panoramax where someone has taken them
  • Touch and hold the map to see the address there, and the land parcel Poland

Safety

Help nearby when every minute counts

The Safety hub in the menu brings together what helps in an emergency and what is worth preparing in advance. The nearest shelter, defibrillator and your walking range are one tap away. When you need help, send your location by text message, or read the coordinates, address and Plus Code out to the emergency operator, even offline.

  • Quick actions: the nearest shelter from the State Fire Service register, the nearest AED defibrillator and the way there, “Call 112” Poland
  • Walking range: how far you can get in 1, 3 and 5 minutes, and which shelters and AEDs are within reach
  • 72-hour emergency kit: a checklist based on the Polish government's safety guide, with reminders when items are about to expire
  • Family plan: home, work, school and meeting points on the map, shared with your family by link
  • Offline map: terrain and a 4-day forecast for an area of your choice without internet, plus shelters, AEDs and IMGW warnings Poland
  • Official channels: emergency numbers, Alert RCB, RSO and siren signals Poland

In an emergency, always call the emergency number first: 112 anywhere in the EU.

Directions

Directions by car, bike or on foot

Compare the options, check the time and distance, then tap “Start”. The app guides you turn by turn and reads the directions aloud.

  • Directions from where you are in one tap
  • Route options, such as avoiding motorways
  • Elevation profile for cycling and walking
  • Save the route as a GPX file

Public transport

When does the next bus leave?

Tap a stop on the map to see the next buses, trams and trains. Pick a trip to see its route and the stops ahead. Departures appear wherever operators publish their timetables as open data.

  • Departures from stops and stations
  • Filter by the lines that call at the stop
  • The trip's route on the map
  • A public transport layer with stops

Mountains

Into the mountains with a trail map

Turn on hiking trails and 3D terrain, and before you set off, check the weather on the summit and the avalanche danger.

  • Hiking trails with a trail card: route, length and waymark
  • 3D terrain, contour lines and hillshading
  • Weather for mountain peaks
  • Avalanche danger and trail closures in the Tatra National Park Poland

Maps and layers

The map you need

Choose a map type: default, terrain, topographic or satellite (aerial photos of Poland). Add layers and open your own route files.

  • Cycle routes, hiking trails, public transport and rain radar
  • GPX, KML and GeoJSON files opened on the map
  • “My layers”: add a map from your own WMS or WMTS address
  • Land parcels, buildings, boundaries and property prices Poland
  • Distance and area measurement, 3D buildings, dark mode

Privacy

Your data stays with you

Open Maps needs no account and collects no statistics. It doesn't know who you are, and it doesn't need to.

  • No account

    No email address, phone number or password to hand over.

  • No ads or tracking

    No ads, visitor counters or profiling, on this page or in the app.

  • Data on your device

    Saved places, search history and settings stay in your browser.

  • Location only when needed

    A data service gets a location only for a feature that needs it, such as directions, the weather or Wikipedia articles nearby.

We spell out which service receives what: About → Privacy.

How to install

You don't have to install anything: the map works straight away in your browser. Add it to your home screen to get its own icon, full screen and a faster start, even offline.

Your device

Android

Chrome, Edge, Samsung Internet

  1. Open the map in your browser.
  2. Tap the menu in the top-right corner.
  3. Choose “Install app” or “Add to Home screen”.
Your device

iPhone and iPad

Safari (other browsers too since iOS 16.4)

  1. Open the map in Safari.
  2. Tap “Share” .
  3. Choose “Add to Home Screen” and confirm with “Add”.
Your device

Computer

Chrome, Edge, Safari on a Mac

  1. Open the map in your browser.
  2. Click the install icon at the end of the address bar.
  3. Confirm with “Install”.

Safari on a Mac: File menu → “Add to Dock”. Firefox doesn't install websites, but the map works in it as usual.

Questions and answers

Is Open Maps really free?

Yes. There are no fees, subscriptions, paid features or ads. You don't need an account or an email address either.

Where does the data come from?

The map, place search and directions use OpenStreetMap, the open map made by its community. In Poland, aerial photos, land parcels, buildings and addresses come from the public services of the Head Office of Geodesy and Cartography (GUGiK), shelters from the State Fire Service register and weather warnings from IMGW, the Polish weather service. Weather forecasts come from Open-Meteo, place descriptions from Wikipedia and street-level photos from the open Panoramax service.

The full list with licences: Data sources.

Does it work offline?

Partly. After your first visit, the app starts without an internet connection, and your saved places, search history, emergency kit, family plan and location sharing (coordinates and Plus Code) work offline.

In “Offline map” (in Safety or Settings) you can save an area, say 10 km around your home: terrain shading and a 4-day forecast, plus shelters, defibrillators and IMGW warnings in Poland, as of the day you download them. The street map and place names can't be downloaded, because the basemap provider (OpenFreeMap) doesn't allow it. Address search and directions need a connection.

Does it work outside Poland?

Yes. The map, place search and directions work all over the world, and the app is in English, with articles from English Wikipedia (switch the language in Settings → “Language / Język”). Some information covers Poland only: land parcels, property prices, aerial photos, shelters, defibrillators, IMGW weather warnings, avalanche bulletins and trail closures in the Tatras. Public transport departures are shown wherever operators publish their timetables.

Does the app know where I am?

Only if you allow it in your browser. Your position is used on your device, for example to show the blue dot. It goes to the routing service only when you plan a route from “Your location”, and to the address service when you share it with an address.

Which devices does it work on?

Current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on Android, iPhone, iPad and computers. The map needs WebGL graphics, which virtually every modern device has.

I've spotted a mistake on the map. What can I do?

The map comes from OpenStreetMap, which anyone can improve: fix the map on OpenStreetMap. To report a bug in the app, write to krz.noga@gmail.com.
